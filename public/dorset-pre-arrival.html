Before you arrive

There is no reception and no key to collect. Read this once before you set off and the rest of the stay takes care of itself.

What to pack

Bring this

  • Food, drink and milk
  • Pizza toppings and tomato sauce — the flour is here
  • Clothes for walking down the hill
  • Warmer clothes for sitting out after dark
  • Crocs or slippers for the deck
  • A torch, if you like wandering off the path
  • Enough mobile data, if you must be reachable

Leave this at home

  • Towels, bathrobes, bed linen, tea towels, oven cloths
  • Pots, pans, crockery, cutlery, glasses, a kettle
  • Salt, pepper, olive oil, sugar, flour, tea, coffee
  • Shampoo, conditioner, body wash
  • Logs, kindling, firelighters
  • Candles — we supply enclosed lanterns
Please don’t bring

Tea lights or unguarded candles. A timber building in a wood is not the place to negotiate on this. There are safety lanterns in every treehouse.

Speakers or music systems. Sound carries a very long way through a wood at night.

Drones. Strictly not, at any time, anywhere on the site.

Stilettos. The boardwalks will win.

Dogs and children. Two adults only, 18 and over.

Packing for the walk

It is five minutes downhill from the car park on woodland paths and boardwalks. Small hard wheels do not enjoy this; a holdall or a rucksack does. Bring what you need for the stay and leave the rest in the car — you can always walk back up for it.

If you are travelling by train, remember that the nearest shop is two miles from the woods and you will need a pre-booked taxi in both directions. Ordering ahead is much easier than solving it on arrival.

Ordering ahead

The woodland shop will take an order up to the day before you arrive. Frozen pizza dough is put in your fridge to defrost on the morning of arrival; everything else is waiting for you inside.

The shop also runs as an honesty cupboard throughout your stay — cider, fizz, dough, extra kindling and firelighters — paid for through the shop website whenever suits you.

Arriving

  1. Park along the hedge at the top. There is a 13-amp trickle charge point for electric cars; no fast charging, as the village supply will not take it.
  2. Walk through the small wooden pedestrian gate on the left and follow the path downhill. Do not drive the track across the meadow.
  3. At the camp centre, move the slate tab with your treehouse name on the in/out board across to ‘in’.
  4. Note the duty phone number posted below the board — it can change during a stay.
  5. Follow the signs to your treehouse. It is unlocked with the key inside. The Woodsman’s door opens on a wheel, turned anticlockwise; Dazzle and Pinwheel have ordinary handles.
The log burner

We cannot light it before you arrive, so it will be laid but cold. The treehouses are very well insulated and come up to temperature quickly. Instructions are in the guest folder and on this site.

What you might hear

We can promise you quiet from us. We cannot promise silence, and it seems fairer to say so now than to have you wonder at 6am.

  • Owls, foxes, deer and a very great deal of birdsong
  • Dairy farming either side of the boundary: herding for milking, machinery, silage and hedge cutting, and occasionally muck spreading
  • Beaters working the neighbouring fields on a handful of winter Tuesdays. No guns in the woods, ever
  • A village party or a wedding, once in a while
  • The railway at the bottom of the valley, depending on wind and leaf cover

Then it is yours

Nobody will contact you during your stay unless we have to. If you do not hear from us, all is well.
